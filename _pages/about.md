Maddy is a 5th year PhD candidate in the wildlife biology program at the University of Montana. She is generally interested in understanding species interactions and their consequences for wildlife populations and disease dynamics. After studying predator-prey interactions in northern Minnesota and in Yellowstone National Park, she started to wonder how parasite species might interact (like little predators) within their shared host. She is particularly interested in how within-host parasite interactions influence host populations and disease transmission over time. Maddy’s research in the Luis Population and Disease Ecology Lab addresses these questions by studying parasite co-infections in the deer mouse-Sin Nombre virus model system. On a typical day, you can find Maddy trapping small mammals in western Montana, identifying and counting deer mouse parasites in the lab, or at her computer writing code for mathematical models that will help her understand the relationships among the co-infecting parasites that she identified in the lab. When she's finished, results from her research will help us to understand more broadly how multiple parasite infections influence disease dynamics in wildlife populations.

Maddy grew up in Minnesota, where she discovered her passion for ecology while exploring the forests and lakes around her home. She received a BS in Ecology, Evolution, and Behavior from the University of Minnesota, with a minor in Fisheries, Wildlife, and Conservation Biology. Her research experiences have spanned a wide range of ecological questions and systems, including wildlife populations in northern Tanzania, wolf-deer predator-prey dynamics in northern Minnesota, swift fox recovery in northeastern Montana, and non-invasive wolf population monitoring in northern Idaho. She has also contributed significantly to research and management initiatives with the Yellowstone Wolf Project where she started her own project digitizing the Park's wolf skull collection. These experiences ultimately led her to her current research on the disease dynamics of a rodent-borne zoonotic pathogen in western Montana. In her free time, Maddy enjoys traveling with her husband, exploring the wild places of Montana, working in the garden, and playing with her dogs.
